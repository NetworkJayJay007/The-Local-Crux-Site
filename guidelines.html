Community Guidelines

Last updated August 20, 2026

Terms of Service · Privacy Policy · Liability Disclaimer · Community Guidelines

The Local Crux exists to help climbers find real, local partners — not to be a dating app, a marketplace, or a place to vent. Everyone here is trusting a stranger enough to meet up and be tied to the same rope, so we ask that you keep the following in mind.

Zero Tolerance for Bigotry and Harassment

Racism, sexism, homophobia, transphobia, or any other form of bigotry — in messages, profiles, or in person after connecting through this app — will result in an immediate suspension. Harassment of any kind (unwanted contact, threats, stalking, sexual harassment) is treated the same way. A second confirmed incident, of any type, results in a permanent ban — we take this seriously so everyone here can feel safe.

Report and Block — Don't Just Tolerate It

If someone makes you uncomfortable or unsafe, you don't have to keep talking to them. Block them — it's immediate and private. If what happened crosses into harassment or bigotry, please report it too, so we can look into it. Reports are reviewed by a real person, not an algorithm.

Climbing Etiquette — What We Ask of Every Partner

  • Communicate before you climb. Confirm belay method, commands, and experience level with a new partner before leaving the ground — don't assume.
  • Never let go of the brake strand. Full attention on your climber, phone away, until they're back on the ground.
  • Check knots and harnesses — yours and theirs. A partner check isn't an insult, it's standard practice. Expect it, offer it.
  • Be honest about your grade and experience. Sandbagging a partner (claiming a harder grade than you climb, or hiding that you're new to lead/outdoor climbing) puts both of you at risk.
  • Respect the crag or gym. Share space at the base of routes, avoid hogging boulders or top-ropes, keep music off or at a volume others nearby can't hear, pack out what you bring in, and follow any posted local ethics (chalk, bolting, closures).
  • Respect a partner's "no," on the wall and off it. If someone decides they'd rather skip a route, a grade, or the plan for the day, that's the end of it — no pressure, no guilt-tripping.

In Short

Show up prepared, communicate honestly, keep each other safe, and treat every partner with respect. That's all we ask, and it's what makes this a place people want to keep coming back to.
